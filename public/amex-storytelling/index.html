Why this programme

Reports and dashboards only matter when someone acts on them.

Most teams already have the data. What decides whether a recommendation is approved is how clearly it is framed, how well it fits the audience, and how calmly it is delivered when questions start.

Participants learn to find the insight in the data, build the story around it, and present it with clarity and confidence. AI is built into the practice day: every participant is evaluated live by AI on the Present with Intelligence platform and receives a personalised performance-gap report.

Learning outcomes

From raw numbers to a message people act on.

The six outcomes follow the path a presentation takes, from the data to the room, and end with the presenter.

  1. Data

    Find the one insight in a spreadsheet.

  2. Clarity

    Turn a dashboard into one clear point.

  3. Structure

    Lead with the answer, then the evidence.

  4. Delivery

    Hold attention from the first word in a distracted age.

  5. Audience dynamics

    The same numbers for a manager, a peer and senior leadership.

  6. Self-belief

    Understand the inner critic and present your insights with authority.

Programme structure

Three days: learn it, then practise it.

Days 1 and 2 are taught. At home, each participant crafts one 8-minute presentation on their own report or dashboard. Day 3 is The Jury: each participant presents the presentation they crafted at home, with questions from the AI mid-presentation and an expert debrief, then a focus group debrief on the most common patterns found in the AI review. A separate presenter's guide sets out each trigger and how to handle it.

Day 1Lecture & role-play

The presenter and the room

The science of the presenter

Grounded in three human sciences.

  • Limitations of self-confidence: where they come from and how to loosen them
  • Barriers to influence: why a strong message can still fail to land
  • Calm when pressure hits: what happens in the body under stress, and how to get back to regulation when triggered
  • The power of internal self-talk
  • From overthinking to readiness: when high standards and self-doubt slow preparation, and how to turn them into confident delivery
  • Know what trips you up: the root causes of the habits that hold strong presenters back
The room
  • What the room can do to your presentation: the most common situations and audience dynamics
  • Welcoming questions and handling interruptions: turning a challenge into a chance to go deeper
  • Defend your numbers: handling “where does this figure come from?” and “this doesn't match my report”
  • Role-play: audience vs presenter
Day 2Lecture, rehearsals & feedback

Storytelling with data, rehearsed on random data

Storytelling
  • Make one number human: a story behind the statisticData becomes memorable when it's made human.
  • Start with why: lead with purpose before the dataBefore showing any numbers, tell the audience why the topic matters to them. People only pay attention to data once they know what it's for.
  • Lead with the answer, then the evidenceGive your conclusion first, then the proof. Managers and senior leaders want the point straight away.
  • Command the room: the delivery skills the best speakers rely onHow you deliver decides whether people listen.
  • The three ways to persuade: credibility, emotion and logicPeople are convinced by who you are, how you make them feel, and whether your reasoning holds.
  • Less is more: designing for attention spanThe less you put on a slide, the more the audience remembers.
Rehearsals and feedback
  • Random data rehearsals: each participant draws an unseen dataset, finds the one insight and presents it as a short storyPractising on numbers they have never seen builds the habit of finding the story fast.
  • Feedback after every rehearsal: from the instructor and the roomEach participant leaves Day 2 knowing what to keep and what to sharpen before crafting their own presentation.

Delivery methodology

Two days to learn, one day to practise.

Three days of classroom delivery: the first two taught, the third given over to practice. The sessions are built around practice: participants rehearse on Day 2 and get feedback, then present to the jury on Day 3. Live presentations are read by the Present with Intelligence platform, so development points come from the AI and the instructor's expert debrief, and the room gives positive feedback.
